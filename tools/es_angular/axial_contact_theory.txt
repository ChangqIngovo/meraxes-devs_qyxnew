AXIAL BRIDGE TIME: AN INDEPENDENT CLOCK AND ITS SPATIAL LIMIT

Scope
-----
Two constant isotropic sources, each q=0.5, are at (0,0,0) and (d,0,0),
d=1.5. Uniform atom density is one. Initial centre-selected paid spheres have
R0=0.6. The cell width is h=4.5/N; N is a multiple of 6, so the midpoint is a
cell centre. ES modulation is disabled. All bins and spatial cells use the
source-centred radial face graph and opaque-until-full capacity rule.

The formula below is an exact clock for formation of the PAID AXIAL BRIDGE
in this discrete model. The earliest three-dimensional six-connected path
agrees with this clock in every measured fixture. The latter agreement is a
numerical observation, not a theorem for arbitrary geometry or source arrays.

1. Why the axis is an independently solvable chain
------------------------------------------------
For a cell on the line between these sources, its transverse coordinates
relative to either source are zero. Both transverse faces on each transverse
axis are outgoing in the Manhattan DAG. Thus there is no incoming transverse
edge: radiation reaching an axial cell can only arrive from the adjacent
axial cell toward that source. Off-axis opacity cannot feed back onto this
chain. Once preceding axial cells are paid, the vacuum face-flux identity
makes the arrival rate q*f_k exactly, where

  r_k = (k - 1/2) h,
  f_k = Omega_square(r_k,h/2)/(4 pi),
  Omega_square(r,a) = 4 atan[ a^2 / (r sqrt(r^2 + 2 a^2)) ].

Angular-bin partitioning sums to this same face flux; the clock is therefore
independent of angular refinement. Any angular ES modulation would alter this
clock and is excluded from the statement.

2. Exact discrete time
----------------------
Let m=d/(2h), and k0 be the largest integer k with (kh)^2 <= R0^2 + 1e-14,
matching the initial-mask convention. Each source independently fills cells
k=k0+1,...,m-1 with atom capacity h^3. At the midpoint k=m both equal-rate
fronts arrive simultaneously, so its capacity is filled at rate 2*q*f_m.
Consequently

  T_h = sum[k=k0+1,...,m-1] h^3/(q*f_k) + h^3/(2*q*f_m).

This is computed independently in axial_contact.py, without invoking the
transport or event solver. In all measured N=24,30,36,48,60,72,96 fixtures,
comparison to the measured first six-connected paid path is recorded in
axial_contact.json. It agrees at floating-point precision.

3. Spatial consistency of this axial event
------------------------------------------
For every point of the square face, 0 <= y^2+z^2 <= h^2/2. Therefore

 h^2/(4*pi*r_k^2) * (1+h^2/(2*r_k^2))^(-3/2)
       <= f_k <= h^2/(4*pi*r_k^2).

Since R0>0, this gives f_k = h^2/(4*pi*r_k^2) * [1+O(h^2)] uniformly
along the finite interval between the initial front and the midpoint. The
individual filling-time error relative to (4*pi/q)*h*r_k^2 is O(h^3),
and summing O(1/h) cells gives an O(h^2) quadrature/face correction.

The main error is instead the centre-selected initial edge and the half-filled
last radial interval. Put M=d/2 and theta_h=(R0-k0*h)/h in [0,1). Apart from
the negligible fixed initial-mask tolerance,

 T_h = (4*pi/(3*q))*(M^3-R0^3)
       + (4*pi/q)*h*[R0^2*theta_h - M^2/2] + O(h^2).

Thus the exact discrete axial bridge time converges with O(h) error to

 T_cont = (4*pi/(3*0.5))*(0.75^3-0.6^3)
        = 1.7247343668207966.

The coefficient oscillates with floor(R0/h). It need not have one sign,
and convergence is not monotonic. At this fixture's parameters its leading
error divided by h lies between -7.0685834706 and +1.9792033718.
This explains why N=30 or N=60 can be worse than adjacent resolutions.

An independent analytic sequence through N=1536 is saved in axial_contact.json;
error/h stays bounded and (error-leading_error)/h^2 stays bounded. This sequence
requires no high-resolution three-dimensional graph run.

4. What this closes and what remains outside its scope
------------------------------------------------------
The large, nonmonotonic contact-time differences at coarse N are fully
explained for the axial event by discrete cell capacities, the initial
voxelised edge, and simultaneous filling of the common midpoint cell.
They are not an angular-resource leakage or photon-bookkeeping defect.
The formula also gives a concrete event-solver verification independent of
its implementation and a spatial-consistency statement for this event.

A general convergence theorem for earliest 3-D connectivity, arbitrary
heterogeneous density, arbitrary sources, or ES-biased topology is not
established here. Finite angular bins retain shadow diffusion, separately
measured by the opaque-screen tests. Global photon conservation and exact
unobstructed face fluxes alone do not imply exact finite-grid geometry.
